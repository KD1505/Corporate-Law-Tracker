Commercial implications Analysis

The CMA examined whether ownership of virtualisation software could disadvantage competing server hardware. It found potential gains from impairing interoperability would not outweigh lost business. The equity/debt bridge and separate debt commitment show why price and financing measures must not be aggregated.

What the lawyers may work on Illustrative

Review software and hardware interoperability evidence, customer contracts, consideration elections and multi-jurisdiction approvals; prepare integration under the final regulatory conditions.

Next milestone

Historical completion; check subsequent company disclosures for integration developments.

Valuation bridge

Documented finding

The CMA expressly identified $69bn as $61bn equity and $8bn debt on the announcement valuation basis.

  • Broadcom and VMware 26 May 2022 release, transaction details and advisers
  • CMA 21 August 2023 clearance and note 3

Consideration and funding

Documented finding

Cash and stock elections were subject to proration. The $32bn debt commitment and 40-day go-shop were disclosed at signing.

  • Broadcom and VMware 26 May 2022 release, transaction details and advisers

Competition reasoning

Documented finding

The regulator considered interoperability and commercially sensitive information; it concluded no substantial lessening of competition.

  • CMA 21 August 2023 clearance and note 3

Mandates and completion

Documented finding

The announcement names Wachtell and O'Melveny as Broadcom counsel, Cleary as regulatory counsel and Gibson Dunn as VMware counsel; the closing filing records completion.
