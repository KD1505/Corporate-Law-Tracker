Commercial implications Analysis

The case illustrates how anticipated competition remedies can change the economic perimeter of a deal. A buyer must test whether any divestment would undermine the investment thesis or financing model.

What the lawyers may work on Illustrative

Carve-out perimeter, transition services, tax structuring, financing commitments and remedy allocation should be negotiated together. Assess whether a required divestment would undermine the economics of the combined foods business.

Perimeter and consideration

Documented finding

The proposed separation excludes India, Nepal and Portugal food operations and specified other businesses. The transaction attributes approximately $44.8bn EV to the transferred Foods business; the $29.1bn illustrative share component uses the announcement reference share price. The $15.7bn cash payment is adjustable at closing. These components should not be confused with the combined company's valuation.

  • McCormick Transaction overview
  • Hogan Lovells Mandate announcement and value

Ownership and structure

Documented finding

The announced Reverse Morris Trust transaction separates the Foods business before combination with McCormick. On closing, existing Unilever shareholders are expected to own 55.1%, Unilever 9.9%, and existing McCormick shareholders 35% of fully diluted equity. The separation agreement addresses intercompany balances and closing adjustments.

Financing and leverage

Forecast / announced expectation

The $15.7bn committed bridge is a financing commitment, not an additional acquisition payment. Permanent funding is intended to combine new borrowing with balance-sheet cash. The parties forecast net leverage no higher than 4.0x at closing and a return to 3.0x within two years; those targets depend on execution and are not achieved results.

  • McCormick Financing and capital structure

Contractual risk allocation

Documented finding

The merger agreement provides a conditional $420m termination payment in defined recommendation-change or competing-transaction circumstances; a separate capped $75m expense reimbursement applies in specified shareholder-approval failure circumstances. These are not unconditional obligations.

  • Unilever Appendix 1, Merger Agreement

Current competition procedure

Documented finding

CMA opened formal Phase 1 on 16 September and on 11 November identified potential substantial lessening of competition. An in-depth referral can be avoided if acceptable undertakings are offered. The published case has not established clearance. The parties' mid-2027 closing expectation remains conditional.

Practical legal workstreams

Original analyst diligence and execution questions. These are not a claim that particular clauses were agreed or that a firm performed these tasks.

Carve-out

Question: What assets, liabilities and contracts actually transfer?

Map exclusions and shared services; test stranded costs, consent requirements and transitional arrangements in IT and distribution.

Documents to examine: Separation and Distribution Agreement; TSA schedules; asset perimeter and IP register.

Tax structure

Question: What assumptions must hold for the proposed Reverse Morris Trust treatment?

Evaluate tax opinions, control and continuity requirements and post-closing restrictions; do not assume UK or US tax treatment from the label alone.

Documents to examine: Tax matters agreement; tax opinions; ownership schedule.

Acquisition finance

Question: How resilient is the capital structure to closing delay or remedies?

Model debt issuance, bridge maturity, interest rates, adjusted cash consideration and a lower earnings base if divestment is required.

Documents to examine: Bridge commitments; capital structure model; financing conditions.

Competition and governance

Question: What remedy would compromise the strategic rationale?

Track remedy proposals separately from management forecasts. Consider approval mechanics, board composition and covenants as the process develops.

Documents to examine: CMA decision and any undertakings; merger agreement; shareholder materials.
