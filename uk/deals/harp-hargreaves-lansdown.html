Commercial implications Analysis

The rollover election permits exposure to private ownership rather than cash exit, but unlisted shares have different liquidity and governance characteristics. A regulated investment platform's value depends on client assets, net flows, pricing and technology investment; sponsor strategy should be assessed against actual retention rather than forecasts.

What the lawyers may work on Illustrative

Review scheme conditions, rollover election restrictions, regulated entity change-of-control and post-delisting governance.

Exit and rollover

Documented finding

The cash acquisition and rollover alternative require distinct consideration and governance analysis; the latter is not equivalent to listed shares.

  • HL / Harp Bidco scheme document 6 September 2024; Part I §2, Part II §7, Part IX §15, Part X
  • HL issuer notice 25 March delisting and 24 March effective date

Cash, dividends and premium reference date

Documented finding

The scheme specifies 1,110p cash plus a 30p dividend. The total 1,140p implies a 54.1% premium to 740p on 11 April 2024, versus 22.2% to 932.8p on 21 May. Different reference dates explain the different premium figures.

  • HL / Harp Bidco scheme document Part I §2

Debt, fees and rollover

Documented finding

The interim senior term facility is £1.75bn; bidder costs were estimated at £123.4m before tax, including £13.7m for legal advice. These are forecast expenses rather than actual consideration. The rollover alternative uses loan notes convertible into private equity on implementation.

  • HL / Harp Bidco scheme document Part II §7; Part IX §15; Part X
