Foundation · Original practice material

Enterprise value, equity value and cash flow

Enterprise value measures the operating business under a specified valuation convention. In a simplified cash-free, debt-free deal, equity value equals enterprise value less debt plus cash. Actual price adjustments depend on the agreed definitions of cash, debt, debt-like items and working capital. EBITDA is not cash flow: capital expenditure, tax and working capital may consume cash.

A business has £120m enterprise value, £35m debt and £5m cash. Ignoring other adjustments, what equity price is implied?

Model answer and reasoning

£120m − £35m + £5m = £90m. The SPA must specify which items count as cash and debt.

Intermediate · Original practice material

Debt, interest rates and covenants

Floating-rate borrowing commonly references a benchmark plus margin. Rate rises can reduce interest cover, increase refinancing pressure and reduce the debt a buyer can service. Fixed-rate debt and hedging may change the impact. A covenant breach depends on the precise financial definitions, test dates and cure rights, not on a headline rate alone.

A borrower has £80m of unhedged floating-rate debt. Assuming immediate full pass-through, what is the additional annual interest from a 25bp rise?

Model answer and reasoning

25 basis points = 0.0025; £80m × 0.0025 = £200,000 before other changes.

UK merger control and remedies

The UK merger regime is generally voluntary, but the CMA may investigate qualifying anticipated or completed transactions. Review can lead to clearance, commitments, deeper investigation or prohibition. Contractual closing conditions, long-stop dates and risk allocation need to reflect the transaction's particular facts and any separate mandatory regimes.

A buyer completes a UK transaction before the CMA reviews it. Which statement is safest?

Model answer and reasoning

A qualifying completed merger may still be reviewed; the Co-operative case provides a concrete illustration.

Public M&A and capital markets

A public takeover and an equity issuance solve different commercial problems. Before planning a public acquisition, establish whether the Takeover Code applies; before a capital raising, identify the market, admission route, offer and applicable prospectus rules. Regulatory timetables, disclosure and execution risk affect the financing plan.

A foreign bidder retains a London law firm to acquire a foreign private company. Does that alone establish Takeover Code jurisdiction?

Model answer and reasoning

The location of an adviser is not a standalone test of the Code’s applicability.

Foundation · Original practice material

How clients make money

Revenue is sales before expenses; profit depends on costs and accounting rules. Operating cash flow also reflects collection and payment timing. A high-growth company can be profitable on paper but short of cash. Ask what customers pay for, what drives costs, how working capital behaves and what regulation constrains the business.

Sales rise but customers take much longer to pay. What can happen?

Model answer and reasoning

Receivables tie up cash. A profitable business may still need working-capital finance.

Private equity and management rollover

Sponsors seek returns through operating improvements, growth, capital structure and eventual exit. Rollover means existing investors or management reinvest some proceeds or retain an interest. Review shareholder rights, dilution, incentives, transfer restrictions and exit arrangements. The investment’s value and the target’s enterprise value are different concepts.

A sponsor buys a majority stake and management reinvests. What is a priority alongside the SPA?

Continuing shareholders need a workable governance and exit framework, distinct from the acquisition terms.

Securitisation and credit risk

A securitisation uses a financing structure in which investors are paid from a pool of underlying exposures, subject to contractual priorities. Analyse asset transfer, security, servicing, cash-flow waterfalls, credit enhancement and insolvency risk. CMBS can expose investors to property cash flow and refinancing risk.

A CMBS issuer sells senior and junior notes. Does securitisation eliminate borrower default risk?

Model answer and reasoning

The structure allocates credit risk rather than making the underlying exposure risk-free.
