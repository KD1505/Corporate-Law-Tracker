Foundation · Original practice material

Enterprise value, equity value and cash flow

Enterprise value measures the operating business under a specified valuation convention. In a simplified cash-free, debt-free deal, equity value equals enterprise value less debt plus cash. Actual price adjustments depend on the agreed definitions of cash, debt, debt-like items and working capital. EBITDA is not cash flow: capital expenditure, tax and working capital may consume cash.

A business has £120m enterprise value, £35m debt and £5m cash. Ignoring other adjustments, what equity price is implied?

Model answer and reasoning

£120m − £35m + £5m = £90m. The SPA must specify which items count as cash and debt.

Intermediate · Original practice material

Debt, interest rates and covenants

Floating-rate borrowing commonly references a benchmark plus margin. Rate rises can reduce interest cover, increase refinancing pressure and reduce the debt a buyer can service. Fixed-rate debt and hedging may change the impact. A covenant breach depends on the precise financial definitions, test dates and cure rights, not on a headline rate alone.

A borrower has £80m of unhedged floating-rate debt. Assuming immediate full pass-through, what is the additional annual interest from a 25bp rise?

Model answer and reasoning

25 basis points = 0.0025; £80m × 0.0025 = £200,000 before other changes.

UK merger control and remedies

The UK merger regime is generally voluntary, but the CMA may investigate qualifying anticipated or completed transactions. Review can lead to clearance, commitments, deeper investigation or prohibition. Contractual closing conditions, long-stop dates and risk allocation need to reflect the transaction's particular facts and any separate mandatory regimes.

A buyer completes a UK transaction before the CMA reviews it. Which statement is safest?

Model answer and reasoning

A qualifying completed merger may still be reviewed; the Co-operative case provides a concrete illustration.

Public M&A and capital markets

A public takeover and an equity issuance solve different commercial problems. Before planning a public acquisition, establish whether the Takeover Code applies; before a capital raising, identify the market, admission route, offer and applicable prospectus rules. Regulatory timetables, disclosure and execution risk affect the financing plan.

A foreign bidder retains a London law firm to acquire a foreign private company. Does that alone establish Takeover Code jurisdiction?

Model answer and reasoning

The location of an adviser is not a standalone test of the Code’s applicability.

Foundation · Original practice material

How clients make money

Revenue is sales before expenses; profit depends on costs and accounting rules. Operating cash flow also reflects collection and payment timing. A high-growth company can be profitable on paper but short of cash. Ask what customers pay for, what drives costs, how working capital behaves and what regulation constrains the business.

Sales rise but customers take much longer to pay. What can happen?

Model answer and reasoning

Receivables tie up cash. A profitable business may still need working-capital finance.

Commercial interview reasoning

Start with a specific, dated fact. Identify the client and its commercial objective, trace the financial or operational impact, identify legal work and risks, then state what evidence could change your view. Avoid pretending that every issue has the same impact on all clients.

A buyer is concerned about a competition remedy. Which response demonstrates the strongest reasoning?

Model answer and reasoning

The best answer links a concrete regulatory outcome to deal economics and legal drafting. This is original practice material, not an employer question.

Private equity and management rollover

Sponsors seek returns through operating improvements, growth, capital structure and eventual exit. Rollover means existing investors or management reinvest some proceeds or retain an interest. Review shareholder rights, dilution, incentives, transfer restrictions and exit arrangements. The investment’s value and the target’s enterprise value are different concepts.

A sponsor buys a majority stake and management reinvests. What is a priority alongside the SPA?

Model answer and reasoning

Continuing shareholders need a workable governance and exit framework, distinct from the acquisition terms.

Infrastructure and project finance

Project finance often relies on cash flows of a project company, with limited or non-recourse structures depending on the agreed financing. Construction, operating, revenue and political risks are allocated across contracts, insurance, security and sponsor support. A final investment decision is not the same as financial close or operational completion.

An offshore wind development has a final investment decision. What can you safely conclude?

An investment decision is distinct from completion, drawdown and commissioning.

UK national security screening

The National Security and Investment Act creates a distinct national security review regime. Certain qualifying acquisitions of control over entities in specified sectors require mandatory notification. Asset acquisitions are not subject to mandatory notification but may be called in. Competition and national security reviews require separate analysis.

An acquisition is cleared by the CMA on competition grounds. Does that establish NSI clearance?

Model answer and reasoning

The regimes serve different purposes and must be assessed separately.

Securitisation and credit risk

A securitisation uses a financing structure in which investors are paid from a pool of underlying exposures, subject to contractual priorities. Analyse asset transfer, security, servicing, cash-flow waterfalls, credit enhancement and insolvency risk. CMBS can expose investors to property cash flow and refinancing risk.

A CMBS issuer sells senior and junior notes. Does securitisation eliminate borrower default risk?

Model answer and reasoning

The structure allocates credit risk rather than making the underlying exposure risk-free.

Schemes of arrangement and deal certainty

A scheme of arrangement is a court-supervised process. A public takeover implemented by scheme typically requires the relevant shareholder approvals and court sanction, alongside satisfaction of applicable offer conditions. Check the transaction documents and the relevant Companies Act and Takeover Code provisions.

A scheme has shareholder support but merger clearance is outstanding. Which conclusion is sound?

Model answer and reasoning

Shareholder support is not the same as satisfaction of all conditions or legal completion.

Legal due diligence and SPA risk allocation

Diligence maps material legal risks to commercial responses. A known issue may justify a price adjustment, specific indemnity, closing condition, covenant or decision not to proceed. Warranties allocate contractual risk subject to the negotiated disclosure and liability framework; they are not a substitute for verification.

A target's material customer contract requires consent for a change of control. What should counsel prioritise?

Model answer and reasoning

Verify the clause and quantify the exposure. Depending on materiality, consent may be a condition to completion or another negotiated protection.

Data and intellectual property diligence

For a software or data business, distinguish ownership of IP from permission to use third-party content. Review licence scope, assignment and change-of-control clauses, data protection duties, processing arrangements, security and relevant statutory commencement. Commercial value may depend on rights that cannot automatically be transferred.

The target uses a third-party data feed under a non-transferable licence. What is the first step?

Model answer and reasoning

The licence and structure determine whether consent is required. Review the actual terms and applicable law rather than applying a universal rule.

Workforce risk and transaction structures

Employment risk can arise from accrued liabilities, incentives, pensions, disputes, consultation and transfer rules. A deal involves transfer of an operating business rather than the company's shares. Which is best?

The factual structure determines the legal analysis. TUPE may apply to a qualifying transfer; review scope, obligations and relevant exceptions.

Commercial contracts: warranties, indemnities and caps

A warranty is a contractual statement; an indemnity can allocate specified losses under its wording. Liability caps, baskets, exclusions, time limits and mitigation provisions require careful interpretation. A contract's exact terms matter more than labels.

Law firm economics and client value

A commercial firm sells expertise, judgment and execution capacity. Fee arrangements may include hourly rates, fixed fees or other structures. Profitability depends on utilisation, staffing, realisation and overhead; a high-value matter is not necessarily a high-margin matter. Clients value predictable scope, risk control and timely outcomes.

A transaction expands unexpectedly. What demonstrates commercial judgment?

Model answer and reasoning

Transparent scope management helps the client make a business decision while preserving quality and the professional relationship.
